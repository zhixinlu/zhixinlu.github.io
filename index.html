About me

A dynamical-systems foundation for intelligence.

My research builds a dynamical-systems and stochastic-process foundation for intelligence. At its heart is a single question: how can one dynamical system — a brain, or an artificial network — come to understand, predict, and control another dynamical system, the world it lives in? I use that foundation in two complementary ways: to design new algorithms and architectures whose computation is dynamics (dynamical AI), and to build models that understand, predict, and control the dynamical world (AI for dynamics).

I'm especially drawn to bio-inspired and physically-grounded computation: architectures derived from how biological neural systems learn, and models simple enough in their dynamics to be realized efficiently in physical, non–von Neumann substrates rather than only on digital hardware. This thread runs from foundational theory to modern architectures — transformers, diffusion models, state-space models, RNNs, reservoir computers, and world models — and back to the neuroscience of how brains represent and forecast a changing world.

Along the way I've helped show that reservoir computers can predict large, spatiotemporally chaotic systems from data alone — work Quanta Magazine covered — introduced reservoir observers that infer a chaotic system's hidden variables, shown recurrent networks inferring global temporal structure from purely local examples (Nature Machine Intelligence), and separated chaotic sources with a literal tank of water — computation carried out by a physical medium. The unifying idea I keep sharpening is invertible generalized synchronization, a mechanism by which one dynamical system learns to embed and predict another, together with a geometric, energy-landscape view of attention that recasts transformers as hopping between basins on a learned landscape.

Two directions, one ring

The logo says it all: AI for Dynamics · Dynamics for AI.

Dynamical AI

Designing learning algorithms and architectures from dynamical-systems principles — recurrent networks, reservoir computers, energy-landscape views of attention, and Hebbian-like plasticity — that learn, generalize, and remain stable.

AI for Dynamics

Building AI that understands, predicts, and controls the dynamical world: inferring models from data (even non-sequential data), forecasting chaos, and separating coupled dynamical sources.

Bio-inspired computation

Architectures derived from how biological neural systems learn, bridging single-neuron dynamics, cell types, and network-level computation.

Physically-grounded learning

Learning machines implementable in non–von Neumann physical substrates — including the celebrated "tank of water" reservoir computer.
