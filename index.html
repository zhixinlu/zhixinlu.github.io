Featured research / October 2026

Can a system generate chaos backward in time?

PASTFUTURE

A question about the limits of stable generation.

Preprint · arXiv:2610.02845

An Arrow of Time in Stable Autonomous Generation

Zhixin Lu · October 2, 2026

Replaying a trajectory backward is easy. Building a system that stably generates that reversed motion is a different problem.

For a class of chaotic dynamics including the classical Lorenz attractor, I prove an obstruction to exact, stable reversed generation by finite-dimensional autonomous systems with C2 vector fields, compact attracting sets, and continuous readouts. Adding latent variables does not remove the obstruction within this class.

About

Zhixin Lu

Scientist at the Allen Institute
Seattle, Washington

Background & CV →

My work brings together nonlinear dynamics, stochastic processes, machine learning, and neuroscience. I earned my Ph.D. with Edward Ott at the University of Maryland and was a postdoctoral researcher with Danielle Bassett at the University of Pennsylvania.

My research has explored prediction of spatiotemporal chaos, reservoir observers for hidden-state inference, learned attractors, and source separation using simulated water dynamics. These studies motivate a broader ambition: to understand the dynamical principles that make intelligent computation possible.
