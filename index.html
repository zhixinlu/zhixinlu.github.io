About me

Bio-inspired & physically-grounded computation.

Back in August 2017, I received my Ph.D. at the University of Maryland, College Park, studying nonlinear dynamics and machine learning under the mentorship of Dr. Edward Ott. I then continued as a postdoc in the Complex Systems Group led by Dr. Danielle S. Bassett at the University of Pennsylvania. I am now a Scientist II at the Allen Institute, studying the intersection between biological and artificial intelligence.

I am especially drawn to bio-inspired and physically-grounded computation: architectures derived from how biological neural systems learn, and learning machines implementable in non–von Neumann physical substrates rather than only digital hardware. This connects foundational theory to modern architectures — transformers, diffusion models, SSMs, RNNs, reservoir computers, and world models — and to the neuroscience of how brains represent and forecast the dynamical world.

Two directions, one ring

The logo says it all: AI for Dynamics · Dynamics for AI.

Dynamical AI

Designing learning algorithms and architectures from dynamical-systems principles — recurrent networks, reservoir computers, energy-landscape views of attention, and Hebbian-like plasticity — that learn, generalize, and remain stable.

AI for Dynamics

Building AI that understands, predicts, and controls the dynamical world: inferring models from data (even non-sequential data), forecasting chaos, and separating coupled dynamical sources.

Bio-inspired computation

Architectures derived from how biological neural systems learn, bridging single-neuron dynamics, cell types, and network-level computation.

Physically-grounded learning

Learning machines implementable in non–von Neumann physical substrates — including the celebrated "tank of water" reservoir computer.
